- A pin can be a name: "mark your home" or a daily route with a start point identifies the respondent. Ask for the area or the destination, not the origin.
- Consent and purpose: say who is asking, what for, how long the data is kept, and whether results will be public, in plain words on the first page.
- Minimise: ask only what will be acted on (rule 10); demographics only when results will be segmented by them; never required.
- Publishing: aggregate, mask small groups, keep free text and photos out of public pages; think about who is visible in uploaded images.
- Sensitive topics (safety, health, housing insecurity) need a stricter line on what is shown on a map, and a way for respondents to answer without mapping.
- Children and young people: parental consent and school settings; map literacy limits (Alderton et al. 2026).
