By the end of this page the survey is open for responses and you have a link, a QR code and an embed snippet to hand out.

## The three statuses

A survey is always in one of these states, shown in the publishing widget at the top of the editor:

| Status | Who can open the link | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | Nobody. The public address answers "unavailable". | Building the survey |
| **Testing** | Only people with your private test link | Trying it on a phone, sending it to colleagues |
| **Published** (**Accepting responses**) | Anyone with the link | Collecting responses |

A fourth state, **Closed**, stops new responses while keeping everything you collected; **Reopen** brings the survey back.

## Test it first

1. In the publishing widget, click **Move to Testing**.
2. Click **Share test link** and send the address to yourself and a colleague. The test link carries a token; the plain survey address stays closed until you publish.
3. Walk through the survey on a phone. Check that the map opens on the right street, that the **Place point** button is understandable, and that the popup questions make sense on a small screen.
4. Fix wording in the editor as you go. While the survey is in testing, every edit is allowed and reaches the test link at once.

Responses you collect through the test link are test responses. You decide what happens to them at the next step.

## Publish

Publishing needs a confirmed email address. Right after you sign up you can build and test for 24 hours without confirming, but **Publish** opens a **Confirm your email to publish** dialog until you open the link we mailed you. The yellow banner at the top of the editor sends a new link if the first one is lost.

1. Click **Publish — open for responses**.
2. In the **Publish Survey** dialog, leave **Delete all test responses (recommended)** ticked so your own trial pins do not end up in the results.
3. If the survey has more than one language and some text is still untranslated, Mapsurvey warns you that those respondents will see the primary language. You can publish anyway or go back and fill the gaps.
4. Click **Publish**.

The widget now reads **Accepting responses**. As long as nobody has answered, **Back to Draft** is still available and undoes the publication. Once responses arrive, structural changes go through a separate draft; see [Edit a published survey](/how-to/edit-a-published-survey/).

## Share the link

1. Open the **Share** tab and choose **Share link & tracking…**.
2. Under **Survey link**, click **Copy link**. This is the address to put in an email, a post or a newsletter. Anyone who has it can respond; no account is needed.
3. Click **QR code** to show a code you can put on a poster, a flyer or a slide. People scan it with their phone camera and the survey opens in the browser.

<!-- screenshot: the Share page with the survey link, the copy button and the QR code shown -->

Two more options on the same page:

- **Embed on your site** gives you an iframe snippet for a page on your own website or on a site builder. The survey works inside the box and responses arrive here as usual. Details, including how to restrict which sites may embed it, are in [Embed a survey](/how-to/embed-a-survey/).
- **Track where responses come from** makes a separate link per channel (newsletter, social media, printed poster). Each response that arrives through a tracking link is tagged with its source, and the **Responses** page compares channels.

## What respondents see

Respondents open the link, pick a language if the survey has more than one, and go straight to the first section. They do not create an account or install anything. If a respondent closes the browser and returns on the same device, their answers so far are kept.

Everything a visitor can do with a link, including passwords and public listing, is set out in [Sharing and access](/reference/sharing-and-access/).
