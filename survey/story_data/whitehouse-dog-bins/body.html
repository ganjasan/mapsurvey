An estate that outgrew its bins

Whitehouse is one of the new neighbourhoods on the western edge of Milton Keynes, still being built out between the old Watling Street and the open fields beyond Calverton Lane. Whitehouse Community Council looks after the parish, and among the things it provides are the dog-waste bins: fourteen of them, most installed when the estate was much smaller.

The goal

Two years after its last review, with new streets added every year, the council had a dog-bin budget for 2026/27 and one question to answer before spending it: where are bins missing? Not as seen from the council office, but as felt by the people who walk the dogs every day. The council wanted a short list of locations it could check against land ownership, access for emptying and the cost of collection, and take to a council meeting.

So instead of guessing, it put a map in front of residents.

In the council's own words

The survey closes with the council's explanation of what happens to a suggestion:

Whitehouse has grown considerably since many of our existing dog-waste bins were installed. We're reviewing whether additional bins are needed and where they would provide the greatest benefit. Your suggestion will be considered alongside responses from other residents and practical factors such as land ownership, accessibility, installation costs and collection arrangements.

From the dog-bin survey's closing page

The survey

Two pages, built in an afternoon. The first is the map: the fourteen existing bins drawn as blue dots, and one task for the resident, to drop a single pin where an additional bin would be useful, then, in a pop-up on the pin, tick why (a popular dog-walking route, too far from an existing bin, dog fouling is a problem here, near a park) and add a comment if they wish. The second page asks who is answering. That is all, and it took residents a median of a minute and a half.

The council posted the link on its website and on its Facebook page on 2 September 2026. Most of the answers arrived that same afternoon.

What came back

In nine days 39 residents answered and 37 of them placed a pin; 38 of the 39 did it on a phone. The pins cluster: the entries to the green corridor through the middle of the estate and the footpaths out to the woods come up again and again, with the same reason attached, a popular walking route with no bin within a few minutes' walk.

I have to say, I am very impressed with the tool. It's intuitive, and has been very well-received.

Councillor Julian Thomas, Whitehouse Community Council

What the council did with it

Within the month the council had a paper in front of it: 37 suggested locations, reviewed against the existing network and the main walking routes, and a recommendation to buy and install six additional bins, concentrated along the green corridor and at the entrances to the woods, with final siting left to the council's warden. The whole proposal fits inside the year's existing dog-bin budget. The map at the top of this page is the council's own: the fourteen existing bins and the six it recommends adding.

Lessons learned

What Whitehouse Community Council found out running its first map survey

01

Say which button to press.

Residents expected to tap the map and missed the button. Twenty-five minutes after launch the council renamed the question "CLICK HERE", and by the evening the introduction was a single instruction.

In Mapsurvey: placing a pin still starts from a button, so a tap on a phone is never mistaken for zooming. We are working on making it obvious without renaming the question.Not yet
02

Show what already exists.

With the fourteen bins on the map, residents filled the gaps: half of the suggestions were 200 metres or more from the nearest bin.

In Mapsurvey: reference layers. Upload a GeoJSON of what exists and it is drawn under every respondent's map.Available
03

Let residents back a pin instead of adding one.

The council's first wish after the survey: a corner asked for by ten people should be one pin with ten votes, not ten pins.

In Mapsurvey: shared map. Residents see each other's pins and support one with a 👍 or a comment, and the export counts the votes.Built for Whitehouse
04

Post twice.

The launch post brought 27 answers in two days; a reshare a week later brought ten more.

In Mapsurvey: a tracked link for each channel, and the survey's Performance tab shows how many people each one brought.Available

What's next

The council is already planning its next map survey, on where winter grit bins should go. One thing Councillor Thomas asked for after the first survey, letting residents support a pin that someone else has already placed instead of adding a duplicate, is now part of every Mapsurvey map survey.
