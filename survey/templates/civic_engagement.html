{% trans "What it is" %}

{% trans "Map-based civic engagement, in plain words" %}

{% trans "Civic engagement is residents taking part in the decisions that shape where they live: a consultation on a road scheme, a participatory budget, a neighbourhood plan, a safety audit, a vote on what the park should become. Community engagement is the same relationship seen from the organisation's side, and a civic engagement platform is the software that runs it. Whatever the label, most of the input a city asks for has a location. The crossing. The dark path. The route to school. The corner where the market should be." %}

{% trans "Map-based community engagement makes that location the answer format. Instead of a text box that asks people to describe a place, the survey asks them to mark it: a point for a spot, a line for a route, a polygon for an area. One or two short questions sit on each mark, so the why travels with the where. The result is community engagement mapping in the literal sense: every contribution is a feature with coordinates, and a hundred contributions are a layer the planning team can open in its GIS, filter, count and overlay on the plan." %}

{% trans "It also widens who takes part. A public meeting reaches the people who can be in a room on a weekday evening. A link on a phone reaches parents, shift workers, students and the residents who would never speak in a hall but will happily draw the route they cycle every morning. Participation needs no account and takes minutes, and the survey text can be shown in 75 languages, which matters in a district where the council's language is not everyone's first." %}

{% trans "Mapsurvey is open source under the AGPLv3 licence and free to run at any scale. A city workspace holds any number of surveys and team members; each survey has its own link, its own results map and its own public results page. The hosted service runs in the United States; a city that must keep residents' data on its own servers runs the same software there." %}

{% trans "How to engage people on a map" %}

{% trans "Four steps that turn a question into a layer" %}

{% trans "1 · BUILD" %}

{% trans "Ask one concrete question per map" %}

{% trans "'Where do you feel unsafe cycling?' beats 'Tell us about cycling'. Choose the geometry that fits the answer, attach a short follow-up question to each mark, and add your own layer, such as the existing network or the draft plan, so people react to something real." %}

{% trans "2 · COLLECT" %}

{% trans "Share a link, not a form" %}

{% trans "A link in the newsletter, a QR code at the bus stop, an embed on the project page. Residents mark the map on their phone and answer the questions on each place. Show earlier contributions on a shared map and let people react to them, so the engagement is a conversation, not a mailbox." %}

{% trans "3 · ANALYZE" %}

{% trans "Count, filter, overlay" %}

{% trans "See every contribution on one map, filter by answer, switch to a heat map, open single responses, and clean out the test runs. Clusters appear on their own; the follow-up answers explain them." %}

{% trans "4 · SHARE" %}

{% trans "Show people their input was used" %}

{% trans "Publish an aggregated results page, export the layer to the GIS for the plan, and run the next round on top of this one. Trust in civic participation comes from the loop closing in public." %}
