{% trans "What it is" %}

{% trans "Where participatory budgeting software usually goes wrong" %}

{% trans "Participatory budgeting gives residents a direct say over part of a public budget: they propose projects, the administration checks feasibility and cost, and residents vote on what gets funded. Most participatory budgeting software is built for the last step. It is a ballot with a budget constraint: pick projects until the virtual money runs out, or drag sliders to balance a budget. That step matters, but it comes months after the step where most PB programmes lose their residents: idea collection." %}

{% trans "Idea collection usually happens in a form. 'Describe your proposal and where it would go.' The answers are paragraphs, the locations are addresses typed from memory, and someone in the council spends weeks geocoding and de-duplicating them before anyone can see that forty people asked for the same crossing. A participatory budgeting map turns that around. Residents put the proposal where it belongs, as a pin, a line or an outlined area, answer the questions attached to it, and add a photo. Duplicates become a cluster, and the cluster is the first result of the programme." %}

{% trans "Mapsurvey is that map. It is a participatory budgeting tool for the proposal and prioritisation rounds: collecting locations, showing them on a shared map so residents can react to each other's ideas with a thumbs up or down and a comment, and exporting the whole set to the GIS and the spreadsheet where feasibility and costing happen. It is not a budget-allocation or voting module. Reactions are one per session and do not verify voters; the binding vote on a fixed budget belongs in a PB voting tool or on paper, and many programmes combine the two." %}

{% trans "PB budgets should fund projects, not software licences. Mapsurvey is free to run, with no per-project or per-response fees, and open source under the AGPLv3 licence, so a city that wants its residents' proposals on its own servers can host the same software itself. The hosted version runs in the United States." %}
