Mapsurvey vs Maptionnaire

Both platforms let you build map-based surveys where participants mark places, draw routes, and outline areas. This comparison evaluates them using the ten-criterion framework from Burnett et al. (2023) — and cites Maptionnaire's own peer-reviewed self-assessment for every claim.

The framework

We evaluate both platforms using the ten evaluation criteria proposed by Burnett, McCall, and Ollivierre in Chapter 1 of Evaluating Participatory Mapping Software (Springer Nature, 2023): ethics, cost, technical level, inclusiveness, accuracy, data privacy, analytical capacity, visualization capacity, openness, and accessibility. This is a deliberately neutral framework applied uniformly across ten mapping tools in the book.

Chapter 4 of the same volume — "Maptionnaire" by Marketta Kyttä, Nora Fagerholm, Vera Helene Hausner, and Anna Broberg — is Maptionnaire's own peer-reviewed self-assessment against this framework. We quote it directly below, criterion by criterion.

TL;DR

  • Mapsurvey wins on cost (free vs. $950–$50 000/year), openness (AGPLv3 vs. proprietary), and data sovereignty (self-hosting)
  • Maptionnaire wins on formally audited accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA), respondent language coverage (43 languages), and deployment maturity
  • Roughly equivalent on ethics, GDPR compliance, EU data residency, and core map-based survey building

2 Cost

The financial model — subscription shape, free tier, hidden fees, self-hosting cost.

"Maptionnaire is not an open-source service. Maptionnaire service has a business model in the background: the activities of a company must be profitable. […] In the Maptionnaire service there are three different product levels that contain a varying set of modules. The Maptionnaire subscription can be purchased either as a set term subscription or as a continuous subscription. Prices vary according to these alternatives, ranging from $950 USD per month for a single project to $50 000 USD per year for enterprise solutions." — Kyttä et al. (2023), Chapter 4, §4.3

Mapsurvey is free for hosted use on mapsurvey.org and free to run yourself under AGPLv3. We do not charge per survey, per response, or per team member.

3 Technical level

Expertise required to create, deploy, and maintain surveys. Lower-barrier tools reach broader audiences; higher-barrier tools offer deeper control.

Both platforms are no-code WYSIWYG editors. Kyttä et al. report a usability study of 21 participatory transport planners (Mladenović et al., 2021) in which Maptionnaire scored 9.33/10 on likelihood to recommend — a strong usability signal. Mapsurvey has not yet been published in a comparable academic usability study.

6 Data privacy

"Maptionnaire platform is designed to accommodate the GDPR legislation. Respondents own their data, and they need to be able to access, modify and withdraw the data they have given. Mapsurvey is GDPR-compliant, EU-hosted in Frankfurt (Render.com), and ships a free downloadable DPA template on our Trust page. The self-host option lets organizations become their own data processor for air-gapped or sovereign deployments.

9 Openness

The degree to which the platform, its code, its data formats, and its integrations are open to inspection, modification, and portability.

"In an ideal world, PPGIS software would be open source and free for all to use. When still developed at the university, the original version of Maptionnaire, back then called the SoftGIS, was published as open-source code, free to use. Mapita Oy developed this software further, first based directly on the initial open-source code. Later, several new versions were developed and published. The current version is the third total rewrite of the application's source code after the spin-out from Aalto University." — Kyttä et al. (2023), Chapter 4, §4.10

Maptionnaire was originally open-source (SoftGIS) at Aalto University. Mapita Oy spun out and progressively closed-sourced the codebase across three full rewrites. Mapsurvey is AGPLv3-licensed on GitHub — the source is public, forks are welcome, self-hosting needs no license fee.

10 Accessibility

How well the platform serves people using assistive technology, low-bandwidth connections, or offline contexts.

"Maptionnaire fills all the requirements of A-level as well as AA-level of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.1). […] Maptionnaire is one of the few digital services in Finland to fulfill all the accessibility requirements." — Kyttä et al. (2023), Chapter 4, §4.11

This is a genuine Maptionnaire strength. Mapsurvey has not yet been formally audited against WCAG 2.1 — we ship standard accessible form controls and semantic HTML, but we make no compliance claim until a third-party audit is complete. If WCAG 2.1 AA is a procurement requirement today, Maptionnaire is the more defensible choice.

Neither platform offers offline data collection. Kyttä et al. note that a standalone Maptionnaire version "would be very expensive" to build; the same applies to Mapsurvey and it is not currently on our roadmap.

Choosing between them

Choose Maptionnaire if:

  • WCAG 2.1 AA compliance is a hard procurement requirement
  • You need respondent-interface support across 43 languages
  • Your budget comfortably absorbs $950–$50 000/year and you want a mature managed service
  • You value an established Nordic vendor with a decade-plus academic research record

Choose Mapsurvey if:

  • Cost dominates the decision — free vs. $11 400/year minimum is a real number
  • Open-source code access matters for audit, research publication, or institutional policy
  • You need self-hosting for full data sovereignty or air-gapped deployment
  • You run many surveys and per-project pricing would compound
