A respondent can now spray-paint an area instead of drawing a polygon. Pick Spray area in the type picker and choose a default brush; the respondent paints with a mouse or a finger, switches between three brush sizes, erases, and paints thicker where they are more certain. It fits questions whose honest answer has no boundary — where does the centre end, where do you feel unsafe, where is it too hot in summer.

Responses shows every cloud in the question's colour as a grid of agreement: darker cells are where more respondents painted, each counted once however long they held the brush. The same grid goes to the public results page, with cells below the anonymity threshold left out, and never a single respondent's paint. Exports carry each cloud as a MultiPoint with its dot count and painted area. Frozen public pages with a map block will ask you to re-freeze once.

Why. The method comes from PPGIS research (Map-Me, Huck et al. 2014): a hard-edged polygon forces a precision people do not have, so they skip the question or draw too much.
